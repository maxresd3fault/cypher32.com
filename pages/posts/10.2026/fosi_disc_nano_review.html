Fosi Audio: DISC Nano Review

10/30/2026

A product that really emphasizes the compact in compact disc.

HiFi on the Go

For the past three years I've been moved out of my childhood home galavanting around the world (hence the delays in updating this website (sorry)). I deeply miss my Sony CDP-397 back on my HiFi rack in my room, not to mention my full collection of CDs. However during these past years I have kept some of my favorite albums with me, occasionally picked up new ones, and have used a few different portable CD players to listen to them. Namely an Optimus CD-3720 and a Sony D-EJ002, both from the 90's. Unfortunately the laser on the Optimus gave out, then after the using the Sony for a few months I gave it to my new girlfriend (whoops).

This left me in the market for a new portable CD player. I could have easily bought something used from the 90's again, however before I did that a different product caught my eye on Instagram. The DISC Nano from Fosi Audio is an exceptionally small CD player that plays the with the disc exposed from the side, complemented by a glass lid so you can see the entire disc during playback. This design (minus the glass) is obviously heavily inspired by the Sony Discman D-88 from the late 80's (shown to the right). The D-88 is quite rare and very expensive on the used market, so Fosi releasing this product makes this exposed disc gimmick much more accessible to a lot of people. But depending how you look at it, it might be far more than just a "gimmick". Due to this design, the device fits in the palms of my (already small) hands. Throwing it in a small cinch bag makes it perfect for travel.

So I decided to buy it. My main reasons being the striking design and also the fact its new, meaning I don't have to worry about the laser dying anytime soon. But mostly the design...

What Do You Get?

In my opinion, you get a very competent and premium feeling device, if anything it surpassed my expectations in the build quality department. Even the packaging almost felt almost like unboxing an Apple product. Fosi (a Chinese company for the record) clearly put in effort to make the device and experience feel premium. The plastics on the device are sturdy, it uses real glass and not plastic for the top lid, and peeking through the gap on the CD tray you can see either a black or white circuit board depending on the device's color. The bottom is also made out of a rubber-like material so it won't slide around on a desk, and behind that is even a MagSafe compatible magnet ring to attach the player to various surfaces or holders. Furthermore, the device weighs a comfortable 210 grams, not feeling too light nor too heavy.

It's probably quite obvious at this point, but when using this player you definitely want to be careful, make sure it has enough clearance and nothing is at risk of bumping into it. Otherwise you risk scratching your exposed disc!

As for the actual important technical specifications:

  • Cirrus Logic CS43198 DAC (Digital to Audio Converter)
  • 800mAh battery equating to ~2 hours of battery life
  • 16-300Ω headphone impedance
  • 3.5mm, optical, and 4.4mm balanced audio outputs
  • Electronic Skip Protection (ESP)

Quite a lot for a device thats 102mm by 102mm big. The sound quality is great from my own testing, I would not consider myself to be a real audiophile, but from my research the CS43198 appears to be a very high quality DAC. But if that's not enough, the DISC Nano also supports being hooked up to external DACs via the optical output (of which Fosi sells many).

My biggest problem with this device is the battery life. The battery is quite small and only 2ish hours of real world use isn't great. For reference, my old Optimus is rated for 7 hours of playback time on two AA batteries. Yikes. Although you could always use the DISC Nano plugged in or even with a power bank.

Another notable distinction with this player and most older portable CD players from the 90's and 2000's is there is no kind of MegaBass, bass boost, or any form of audio enhancement. Not everyone cares about these features, but personally I was quite a fan of MegaBass on older Sony devices, otherwise known as E-BASS on my old Optimus.

My final nitpick would be repairability. I do not know how the device comes apart, but the bottom rubber piece appears to be simply glued in. There are two torx screws on the back, but I don't know what they actually hold in place. If I had to guess, theres likely more screws under the glued in rubber piece, plus plastic clips holding it together. But that is my own speculation. The point is, it does not look like you would be getting this device apart and back together without damaging/scuffing it up in some way. So you can likely forget about replacing that (probably soldered) 800mAh battery in a few years down the road. This is unfortunate, but not entirely unexpected. It's also worth noting most of the portable CD players from the 90's and 2000's weren't exactly easy to repair either, but at least they didn't rely on internal rechargeable batteries. However, presumably when the battery in the DISC Nano gives out, you could continue using it plugged in.

Final Thoughts

For $99.99 in the US, or €109.99 in Europe, I personally think this device is worth the price brand new. You can definitely find quality equipment from the 90's or 2000's used for cheaper, but you will be missing out on the Fosi's small size, exposed disc, and modern DAC. It's not perfect and there are some tradeoffs compared to older equipment, but overall I am just happy to see companies like Fosi putting out new CD products in 2026. Us consumers voting with our wallets and telling companies we still want new CD products is what will keep the CD supply chain alive.
